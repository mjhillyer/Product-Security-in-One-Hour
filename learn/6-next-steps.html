Module 6 · 4 minutes

Your next steps

Product security is a team sport, and most of it happens in ordinary work: design reviews, supplier choices, customer conversations and release planning. The best way to make this hour stick is to use it on something real in the next few weeks.

The hour in six lines

1

Security is patient safety

A weakness can become a hazardous situation. Integrity and availability of therapy usually matter most.

2

Design it in

A few principles, applied early, prevent most problems. Know what software you ship: keep an SBOM.

3

The rules converge

FDA, the EU and other regulators expect secure design, risk management, testing, an SBOM and a plan for vulnerabilities.

4

Release is the start

Monitor, accept reports, assess risk to patients, fix, tell customers, and plan for end of support.

5

Everyone has a role

Know the triggers for bringing in product security, and report suspected vulnerabilities the same day.

6

Keep it current

Regulations, components and threats change. Revisit your design, your SBOM and your plans as they do.

Your first 30 days

Week 1

Find your product security team and your organization's channel for reporting a suspected vulnerability. Save both.

Week 2

Check one product or project against the triggers in the roles and engagement guide. Raise anything that applies.

Week 3

Ask whether your product has a current SBOM and an end-of-support plan. If you lead a team, draft your RACI with the RACI template.

Week 4

Run the tabletop exercise kit with your team, or share this course with one colleague in another role.

The Your First 30 Days guide has a fuller plan for each role, and one for teams standing up or improving a product security practice.

Keep learning

  • FDA, Cybersecurity in Medical Devices: Quality Management System Considerations and Content of Premarket Submissions. FDA's current premarket guidance.
  • FDA, Postmarket Management of Cybersecurity in Medical Devices (2016). Controlled and uncontrolled risk after release.
  • IMDRF, Principles and Practices for Medical Device Cybersecurity (2020). The international baseline, with companion documents on legacy devices and SBOMs.
  • The EU's MDCG guidance, including MDCG 2019-16 on cybersecurity for medical devices.
  • Standards: IEC 81001-5-1 (secure lifecycle for health software), ANSI/AAMI SW96 (security risk management) and ISO 14971 (risk management).
  • Health Sector Coordinating Council resources, including the Joint Security Plan and model contract language, and CISA Secure by Design.
